**Morning — Nine Coppers**

Aldric wakes in the Broken Antler to Mira counting their entire fortune: nine copper coins. The room is paid through today only. After that, it's the fish-gutting docks. **The Garrison Board**

They trudge through the rain to check postings. The board is a graveyard of bad opportunities: rat-clearing (three coppers, bring your own poison), an escort job that pays in meals, a two-silver bounty on a pickpocket that's been posted for three weeks, and a wizard-wanted job to exorcise a ghost cat for one silver and a basket of turnips. Mira declares she hates the city.

**Market District**

Walking back through the market, Mira stops at a fletcher's stall and stares longingly at a silk-and-sinew bowstring she can't afford. Aldric mentally notes it for later — if they ever have money, he'll buy it and leave it on her bow without saying a word. She'll punch him for being sentimental. He also watches a blacksmith using a rare northern folding technique on a short sword until Mira threatens to insert the glowing blade "somewhere creative."

**The Card on the Board**

Back at the Broken Antler, Aldric notices a card on the tavern's own board he'd overlooked for five days: *"Discrete procurement services — inquire at bar."* The innkeeper says a big scarred man comes in every evening at sunset, orders whiskey, sits, and waits for people to ask about it.

**Grent and the Contract**

At sunset, Grent arrives — wide as a wardrobe, face mostly scar tissue, oilskin cloak. He lays out the job: a Merchant Consortium supply caravan was hit by goblins on the Ashenmoor Road. A wagon of sealed alchemical reagents is sitting in the old Greymire mine, guarded by fifteen to twenty goblins. Twenty silver each, half up front, plus two-silver bonus per sealed crate recovered. He sketches a map on a napkin — entrance, main shaft, junction at fifty paces, storage shafts angling down.

Mira grills him on the cargo (volatile, toxic, and corrosive alchemical compounds — don't open, drink, or sniff them) and the tactical reality (goblins underground with no sightlines for her bow). Grent warns them about a previous pair of sellswords — one spent three days thinking he was a chicken, the other permanently lost the ability to taste salt.

They take the job. Ten silver advance each. Mira kicks Aldric under the table when he tries to agree to go *tonight* — she insists on tomorrow to rest first.

**Sword Sharpening**

After Grent leaves, Mira goes upstairs to sleep. Aldric stays in the common room sharpening both swords — his longsword (good Thornwall steel, nicked from the rat job they never mention) and Mira's short sword (the cheese-smelling one, blade-heavy, taken off a dead bandit who used it for cheese with more enthusiasm than hygiene). Day 2 — "The Mine, the Wound, the Potion, the Dream"

The day of the mine job, the disaster, Ysolde's vial, and the prophetic dream.

---

**Pre-Dawn — Gear Check**

Aldric and Mira wake before dawn, both pretending to sleep. They banter about her glowing eyes ("Half-elf eyes don't glow. They reflect ambient light. There's a difference."). Cold breakfast from Mira's travel kit — she forces him to eat despite his nerves. They lay out gear on her unused bed: his longsword, leather armor, rope, lantern, belt knife; her longbow, twenty arrows (four culled for imperfect fletching), and the cheese sword she picks up "with the expression of a woman lifting a dead rat by the tail."

They review the plan — get in, grab crates, get out, don't die. Mira warns against heroics, citing the Thornwall rat incident. Aldric's face does "the thing" where his jaw sets and his eyes go distant, imagining a dramatic last stand. She makes him say "no heroics" twice and mean it the second time.

**Undercity Market — Supplies**

They descend into the underground bazaar — vaulted cellars lit by oil lamps, glow-jars, and shafts of sunlight through street grates. Stalls everywhere: dried herbs, secondhand weapons, mysterious substances. Mira haggles an apothecary down from six silver to five for two healing draughts, deploying her negotiation skills ruthlessly. They also buy wound salve (one silver) and bandages.

On the way out, Mira spots fire-enchanted arrowheads — broadheads that cauterize on impact, five silver for three. She stares for three burning seconds, fingers twitching, then walks away without looking back: "Beautiful work. Maybe next time." Aldric mentally notes the stall, planning to buy them for her with the cargo bonus.

**Afternoon — Waiting**

Back at the Broken Antler. Aldric can't nap — his brain plays worst-case scenarios while the ceiling water stain shaped like a horse judges him. Mira sits by the window oiling her bow with meditative calm, explaining her philosophy: "Panic is a resource you spend before you need it and then can't access when you do."

Aldric asks about her worst job. She tells the Vaelith story — hired to escort stolen goods, tracked by six full-blooded elven trackers through a forest. Three days sleeping in a creek bed, eating raw fish. She killed two on the second night. One was a friend of her mother's who'd fed her dinner when she was eight. "I recognized the way he fell." The weight of the conversation — the things you do to survive sitting at your table eating your sleep — bonds them further.

**Lenna at the Bar**

Aldric goes downstairs and runs into Lenna during her off-hours — dressed simply, drinking tea, looking younger and more real without her professional costume. They share a warm conversation. She reads his pre-job tension instantly ("the same expression every sellsword gets the day of a dangerous job — tight around the eyes, jaw set a fraction too hard"). She squeezes his hand and tells him to come back alive. She clocks the Mira situation: "Your business partner. The one who's definitely not just a business partner."

**The Mine — Night**

Two hours after sunset, they leave. Mira takes point through the dark forest with her half-elf eyes while Aldric sinks knee-deep in mud twice and loses a boot momentarily. They smell the goblin camp from thirty paces — rotting meat, unwashed bodies, pipe fungus like burning hair in vinegar.

They slip past a sleeping sentry and enter the mine. Warm, heavy air. Firelight from the goblin camp deeper in. At the junction, goblin markings point to storage. They take the side tunnel — slick, narrow, fungal film on the floor. They find eight crates behind mining canvas: four sealed, two open with intact vials, two gutted. Six recoverable.

Then the horn sounds. The sentry woke up. Goblins mobilize — coordinated footsteps, a second horn blast from the junction above. They know exactly where Aldric and Mira are.

**The Fight Out**

Aldric decides to fight toward the light rather than deeper into the dark. They hit five goblins at the junction — Aldric takes two with his longsword, Mira puts arrows through two more at ten paces (in a tunnel, her arrows punch rather than arc), the fifth flees screaming. More pour from side tunnels — six from the left, three from an alcove, two dropping from a ledge overhead.

The tunnel becomes a meat grinder. Aldric fights tight — choked-up grip, short arcs, jabs, pommel strikes. Mira switches between bow and short sword at his back, loosing arrows over his shoulder then drawing the blade for close work.

Twenty paces from the exit, the floor collapses. Rotten mining timbers give way. Aldric falls into a drainage pit — right ankle jams between shattered beams, a jagged timber splinter drives deep into his calf. His sword splashes into a foot of stagnant water. Pain is instantaneous, white, and total.

Mira drops in, decapitates a goblin peering over the edge, then uses her whole body as a lever to shift the beam. Aldric wrenches free with an involuntary scream. They kill two more goblins in the pit, climb out, and face five more between them and the exit.

Mira props him against the wall and fights all five — two with arrows, two with the cheese sword at the tunnel's narrowest point, and the last is a goblin sergeant in crude armor with a human-skull helmet. It gets past Mira and comes for the injured prey. Aldric parries a neck slash, headbutts the thing (goblins have surprisingly solid noses), and runs it through under the armor. The sergeant folds forward over his blade with a quiet, almost surprised sound.

They escape into the forest. No crates recovered. The job is a total failure.

**Ysolde — The Cistern Witch**

Fifty paces from the mine, Aldric's ankle is twice its normal size, deep purple-blue. The calf wound still has rotten wood embedded in the muscle, edges already unnaturally hot. Temple healers are too expensive. Mira knows of an underground healer — Ysolde, who operates out of the cistern district.

Mira half-carries Aldric back to Valdmere through the south gate (cheaper bribes). The cistern district is the lowest, dampest point of the Undercity — streets perpetually wet, buildings sagging over ancient sealed cisterns.

Ysolde answers the door — tall, thin, iron-gray braid, eyes so dark iris and pupil merge. A bracelet of tiny colored vials clinks at her wrist like wind chimes. Her workshop is a converted cellar: stone walls, surgical instruments on white cloth, shelves of bottles and herbs, a bone saw Aldric hopes is decorative.

She extracts eight fragments of rotten timber from his calf with forceps over ten excruciating minutes. Stitches the wound with twelve meticulous sutures. Packs his ankle in a green poultice. Diagnoses: severe sprain, partial ligament tear, two to three weeks recovery.

Then the vial. Small dark glass, liquid inside black-green and seemingly alive — shifting when tilted. She calls it a "restorative compound" — anti-infectious, anti-inflammatory, promotes tissue regeneration. When Aldric asks what's in it: "Things you wouldn't recognize if I named them."

He drinks it. Bitter, then sweet, then warmth spreading through him like liquid sunlight — a lot of sensation, something mapping him from the inside. Ysolde watches with an unreadable expression: patient, evaluating, "like a gardener watching a seed."

When he asks what effects to expect beyond infection-fighting, she gives him her small closed-lipped smile: *"Healing. The body knows what it needs. The compound simply... facilitates."*

Total cost: eight coppers. Suspiciously cheap.

Walking out, Aldric feels something beyond the wound treatment — a deeper warmth in his core, reaching into parts of him that have nothing to do with the calf injury, touching something fundamental and rearranging it without permission. He dismisses it as exhaustion.
